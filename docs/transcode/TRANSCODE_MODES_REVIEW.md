# Transcode mode policy v1

`transcode_media` and `transcode_batch` accept `mode=size|quality|x265_preserve` through the same action engine used by UI and MCP. Requests without `mode` retain their legacy behavior. Mode requests reject profiles, priority, quality/size controls, custom metrics and preservation overrides. Replacement authorization remains separate; filesystem operations create candidates and never replace originals.

Each operation freezes `transcode-modes-v1`, the complete sampled policy and its SHA-256 digest. Reload verifies the persisted content against that digest. A batch child verifies its parent, manifest membership, file path and mode before inheriting the frozen policy. Plans include mode, policy digest and final size policy in their own digest.

| Mode | Mean target/minimum | P5 minimum | Worst 5-second window | Final savings | Encoder |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| size | 92 / 88 | 84 | 85 | at least 15%, no growth | measured supported x265 / VideoToolbox |
| quality | 96 / 95 | 93 | 92 | at least 15%, no growth | measured supported x265 / VideoToolbox |
| x265_preserve | 96 / 95 | 93 | 92 | at least 0%, no growth | libx265 only |

CAMBI 6/16 limits are inherited initial guardrails: the fixtures (peak at most 3.283) do not calibrate those exact boundaries.

All modes use VMAF model alias `v1_1080p_3h` (`vmaf_v1.0.16_3d0h`), enforced full-reference CAMBI mean <=6 and peak <=16, and sampled final-candidate validation. These are conservative sampled policy gates, not a guarantee of visual transparency or pixel identity. Sources already HEVC are omitted only in `x265_preserve`. Resolution, source depth/color and existing stream preservation checks remain mandatory. Supported source classes are one 8-bit or 10-bit SDR video stream below 45 fps, or progressive 45-61 fps on a worker advertising `quality.hfr_temporal_decimation` (VMAF then measures a frame-exact 2:1 decimation; see PERCEPTUAL_QUALITY_VALIDATION.md). On other workers such sources are skipped as `hfr_quality_unavailable`. Native Main10 additionally needs the worker's execution-tested native quality capability; HDR/Dolby Vision and unsupported classes preserve the original before encoding.

The common optimization selector filters quality eligibility and exact unrounded byte gates first. `size` contests all passing plans. The strict modes restrict to the allowed score margin of the best measured score. Within a fixed 2% band of the smallest qualifying byte estimate, it prefers measured lower sample-encode duration, then stable candidate ID. Unknown time never means faster. Numeric CRF/quality values are not compared between encoders. Estimates remain estimates; worker validation before publication and independent coordinator acceptance enforce actual size and evidence identity.

Mode batches measure each source rather than inheriting another source's sample quality. There is no representative veto of an unmeasured season. This deliberately trades representative reuse for source-specific evidence. The search is bounded to three 10-second windows and at most six configurations: x265 CRF 20/23/26, plus supported VideoToolbox quality 65/70/75 for the first two modes. A hardware-only worker can use its three measured plans. `x265_preserve` never substitutes hardware. Mixed requests and Main10 are tested with fake capabilities and typed candidate validation; this is not a hardware performance claim.

A source search receives 600 seconds of runner wall time, beginning after worker staging/queue admission; its start is persisted and restart cannot reset it. Size mode's frozen policy adds a search extension (see below) that lets a search run past those 600 seconds while it keeps finishing work. A batch has no shared operation cap: its search cost is bounded by the per-source budget times the files and attempts it admits, and its parallelism by `max_parallel_jobs`. (Frozen policies still carry `operation_budget_seconds: 3600` so their digests do not change; it no longer limits admission, and a batch stopped by it before this change resumes by itself.) The parent persists a maximum reservation before starting each source, retains reservations for uncertain accepted work or interrupted search invocations with unknown prior cost, and settles authoritative measured search time to release unused allowance. A restarted search with unknown cumulative cost omits that measurement and keeps its full reservation; the reservation is a conservative allowance, not reported measured CPU or runtime. This measurement includes synchronous sample I/O, probes and quality evaluation, and is not CPU utilization. Queue and human decision waits do not consume that search allowance: a file waiting for a decision before submitting a search is settled at zero, and its reservation reopens if it resumes. A measured search is settled at its measurement, capped at its reservation (the hard cap of its search extension, else its base budget). A changed goal or a new attempt reserves under its own attempt key. Existing same-source unsuccessful-search history also prevents changing action IDs or recipes from silently restarting repeated negative searches. Explicit retries remain reviewed operations. Exhaustion gives attention with no conclusion for unmeasured files. Search timing is not full-file encode timing.

**Search extension.** The 600 seconds are wall time, so a worker that is also running other encodes, even just the batch's own second search, can spend them on CPU contention and end a search that is not hard, with only "Finite search budget exhausted". In a measured batch of one-hour 1080p episodes of 2-4 GB searched two at a time, 600 seconds covered roughly 6-9 of the 15-17 planned sample encodes (each followed by its quality passes) of a larger source, and two smaller sources were cut off during the final verification of a winner that had already been found. Size mode therefore freezes `size.search_extension` (`max_seconds: 2400`, `steady_seconds: 300`, `verify_seconds: 600`) in its policy:

- Past the 600-second base the worker keeps the search going only while a unit of real work (extracting a sample, encoding a candidate sample or measuring its quality; never a skipped one) completed within the last 300 seconds (a single unit took about 10 to 60 seconds in the measured batch; the slower presets of the fallback family can take a few times that). This follows the actual completion rate, so it is throughput-aware without projecting a finish time: the unit total includes fallback candidates and extra windows that an automatic run usually never reaches, so it cannot be used to project one. The check runs about every 10 seconds, so a stall is cut 300 to 310 seconds after the last unit; a search in which no unit at all finishes within the window, for example when one long encode is the only work left, ends there, which a larger frozen window would have to address.
- It stops 2400 seconds after the persisted search start whatever the progress. 2400 seconds is the measured cost of the largest searches (initial candidates, the fallback family and two verification attempts) with margin.
- A search that has begun verifying its winner on the holdout windows (the `verifying_samples` stage) may run `verify_seconds` beyond that cap, so a winner found just before the cap is not discarded unverified. Once verification has started the allowance covers the rest of the automatic flow: result selection and, if the first verification fails, the one fallback family and second verification attempt. The steady rule still applies, so a verification that stalls is stopped. The longest any search can run is therefore 3000 seconds from its persisted start, a hard maximum a restart cannot extend.
- The base budget is unchanged for searches that make no steady progress. Reservations use the longest a search can run (3000 seconds), because that is the most it can take; they are bookkeeping, so a worker without the capability, which still stops at 600 seconds, is reserved the same.

- Existing operations are unaffected. The field is omitted from snapshots frozen before it existed, so their `size-v3` digests still verify and they keep the fixed 600-second budget. New snapshots carry the field and therefore a new digest under the same version string, as the `audio_only_fallback` addition did. A batch's items share its frozen policy, so only a batch created (or re-frozen by a goal change) after this change gets the extension.
- The coordinator sends `search_extension` only to a worker that advertises `search_budget_extension`. Workers reject unknown request fields, so an older worker is never sent it and keeps the fixed budget. The capability is part of the worker's capability fingerprint, which the server recomputes from the fields it knows and fails closed on a mismatch. Upgrading the server first is therefore the compatible order for this change: a new server with an older worker matches and simply omits `search_extension`. Upgrading the worker first works too, but until the server is upgraded it cannot verify the new worker's fingerprint and job submissions fail with a capabilities error (nothing runs wrongly). The same holds for every earlier capability flag.
- Quality, x265-preserve and compact modes keep the fixed budget.
- A search that ends at its budget while another job held a worker slot for at least half of the time it was observed reports `search_worker_busy`. The wait is still `search_budget_exhausted` with only a cancel option, and nothing restarts by itself, but its reason says the worker was busy and that trying again when it is idle is reasonable, and the file is not blamed. The check samples every ten seconds and counts only other jobs and benchmarks whose runner process is alive, so a stale record left by a crash does not count.
- Cost: a search that never finishes can now hold its worker slot for up to 3000 seconds instead of 600 if it keeps completing units. Unit totals include fallback candidates and extra windows, so completion cannot be projected from them; the hard maximum bounds the cost instead. The sizing is a measured estimate, frozen with the policy; changing it later takes a new policy digest.

**Automatic search rules.** In a measured batch of 1080p H.264 episodes, size searches ended with no result although discovery candidates saved 86-88%. Each failure followed the same path: the smallest passing setting failed its holdout windows on quality, the fallback still chose the savings family (more compressed slow settings) because discovery had measured passing quality, the second verification went to an even more compressed setting, and the search ended with safer settings (for example medium CRF 22 with p5 88.1 and worst window 89.3) never verified. Winners that cleared the p5 minimum by a fraction of a point also tended to fail the full-file validation after a long encode. Size mode therefore freezes `size.automatic_search` (`selection_margin_vmaf: 1.5`, `holdout_windows: final_grid_edges`, `quality_fallback_presets: [medium, slow]`):

- A size winner's VMAF p5 and worst-window mean must clear their minimums (84 and 85) by 1.5. A thinner result is labelled `quality_vmaf_selection_margin_not_met` and counts as a quality rejection for pruning and for the fallback choice. The thresholds themselves are unchanged. (Snapshots that verify on the final grid freeze no margin; see "No selection margin when the search verifies on the grid" below.)
- After a holdout fails on quality, every setting at or above that CRF (any x265 family) is ruled out, the second verification goes to the safest remaining measured setting that is still eligible (lowest CRF of the winner's family, not the next-smallest one, which tends to fail the same way), and the fallback (if needed) is the quality family. The same holds when the quality fallback is measured after the failure: the retry goes to its highest-quality eligible setting. Quality limits, the selection margin and the ruled-out set are unchanged, and the scores and bitstream do not change. A holdout that fails only on savings keeps the earlier behaviour. At most two candidates are verified under the earlier rules (see Grid verification below for the rules new snapshots freeze).
- The holdout windows are the first and last positions of the final validation grid instead of 35% and 65%, so the start and end of the source are checked before a full encode. Discovery stays at 20/50/80%. A source too short for that grid keeps 35/65%.
- The quality fallback tries x265 medium CRF 16/18/20 first and slow CRF 16/18/20 only if medium finds nothing (the ladder is 14 candidates instead of 11). The savings family stays slow.
- A candidate that passed discovery but was never verified is labelled `not_verified` (earlier `verification_not_passed`, which read as a failed verification).

The coordinator sends `automatic_search` only to a worker that advertises `automatic_search_rules`; an older worker gets the earlier request. Snapshots frozen before the field existed keep their digest and the earlier rules. Deterministic replays of the recorded failure shapes are in `internal/transcodeworker/benchmark_automatic_replay_test.go`; a live benchmark on the worker has not been run yet.

**Grid verification (`holdout_windows: final_grid`, `verify_until_exhausted`, `final_fresh_windows: 2`).** A search verified its winner on two holdout windows and then lost the full encode to final validation, which measures 5 to 12 windows of 10 s every 600 s. Across 13 recorded size runs on hard 1080p episodes, the pooled final VMAF mean differed from the search's by about 1 point (median), and in 6 of 13 the worst final window sat at a grid position no search window covered, 3 to 7 points below the search's worst window. At least 56% of the final windows were never searched. The data cannot show whether the gap is only positional: the records hold pooled search statistics and one worst final window, never the per-window scores. This change therefore verifies on the same windows and records both sides.

- **Same windows.** For a source whose final grid has 5 or more windows, the search windows are the final grid: the three interior windows nearest 20/50/80% are the discovery windows and every other grid window is a verification window. A verified candidate has therefore been measured on every window final validation will use (discovery plus verification evidence), instead of on two edge windows. Shorter sources keep 35/65% (no fresh windows). The request carries up to 12 windows (`final_grid`); the worker's sample-count check accepts 5 to 12 only for this value.
- **Fresh windows report, they do not gate.** Final validation adds up to two windows that no search or verification window covered (`final_fresh_windows: 2`; none when the source has no room). They are measured after the grid and pooled separately (`fresh_vmaf`); the verdict, the limits (VMAF mean 88, p5 84, worst window 85, CAMBI 6/16) are unchanged and are decided on the grid windows only. They do not gate because the limits were calibrated on grid statistics, so a gate over two extra windows would be a new, stricter limit with no calibration behind it; they exist to show whether a verified result generalizes. A fresh window that cannot be measured is dropped, not a failure.
- **No two-attempt cap.** Verification continues until a candidate passes or none is eligible, inside the search's existing time limit (2400 s, plus the 600 s verification allowance): the smallest eligible setting first, then, after a quality failure, the safest eligible setting (the earlier rule), and once a setting has passed, a bisection between the passing and the failing setting looking for a smaller one that also passes. `not_verified` now means the time limit ended the verification: the decision carries `verification.stopped_by = time_cap` and the candidates' uncertainty says so. A search that has a passed setting keeps it when the limit hits. A search the limit cuts off with nothing verified completes on the worker with its evidence and reports `search_budget_exhausted` (and whether the worker was busy), so the action waits for a decision as for any search that runs out of time; it does not fall back to audio only and does not end as `no suitable candidate`.
- **Reporting.** The result carries `automatic_search.verification` (each attempt, its role and outcome, the pooled scores), `automatic_search.winner_windows` (the winner's score on each search window) and, after the encode, `quality_evidence.windows` and `fresh_vmaf`. `action_status.transcode.windows` (and the job detail) put the search and final score of every window side by side by start time; a final window no search window covered is `unsearched`, an out-of-sample one `fresh`.
- **Cost (estimates, not measurements).** Taking about 30 s per window per candidate (600 s covers three discovery windows of about six candidates), a 45-minute episode (5 grid windows) verifies 2 windows per attempt, as before; a 60-minute one 3 windows (about +0.5 min per attempt); a 105-minute film (11 windows) 8 windows (about +3 min per attempt). Attempts beyond the second add the same again, bounded by the existing search limit (no new cap). Fresh windows add two VMAF windows to final validation (about +0.5 min). Final validation stays mandatory and keeps its limits.
- **Policy and compatibility.** The three rule fields are `omitempty`. Snapshots frozen with the grid-edge holdouts re-hash to the digests they were frozen under and keep the two-attempt search: `sha256:33812f4b...80ee` (`allow_hardware=false`), `sha256:1b73a704...ead6` (media engine on, without the extra margin) and `sha256:bc2e370b...e03` (media engine on, frozen before the fallback rungs). Snapshots frozen next had the grid rules and the margins, with these digests under the same version string: `sha256:6e519947...8ba8` (`allow_hardware=false`), `sha256:206ca220...03e7` (media engine on, with the hardware extra margin) and `sha256:ab4a33d9...6128` (media engine on, without the fallback rungs); new snapshots drop the margins and have newer digests (see below). Snapshots frozen with the extra margin but before the grid keep `sha256:e5ec773d...4264`. So a new `allow_hardware=false` snapshot is no longer byte-identical to the one frozen before this change; every earlier one is unchanged. A worker advertises `automatic_grid_verification`; that capability is part of the fingerprint the server recomputes, so **upgrade the server first**: a new server sends a worker without the capability the edge-holdout request (no new rule fields, `final_grid` downgraded to `final_grid_edges`, no fresh windows), while a new worker with an older server fails the fingerprint check until the server is upgraded. Rolling the server back after new snapshots are frozen drops the rule fields, so those snapshots no longer re-hash; wait until those operations finish. The same-source search review (`roundComparable`) treats rounds under different verification regimes as not comparable, so earlier edge-holdout rounds neither block nor count for a grid search.
- **Interplay with the sample cache and the x265 continuation.** Verification windows are measured through the same pipeline as discovery windows, so the worker's per-window sample cache serves and fills them too (entries are keyed by source, window, encoder parameters and metric settings; a source searched before the grid layout misses for the windows that moved, which costs only that first run). The x265 continuation of a failed VideoToolbox winner is planned on the same grid, so the continued request has the same samples, quality policy and automatic-search rules as the first search, which is what the worker's reuse rule requires: reuse stays limited to candidates that failed a quality gate at discovery and to the discovery stage, and the continued search still verifies on every grid window. The failed VideoToolbox result keeps its per-window search and final scores (`hardware_continuation.windows`), shown next to the x265 result.

**No selection margin when the search verifies on the grid (`selection_margin_vmaf: 0`, no `extra_selection_margin_vmaf`).** Across 13 recorded size runs the 1.5 margin did not predict the final validation result: of the winners that cleared it, 5 passed and 4 failed, and the thin ones split 3 passed and 1 failed. The final check fails on windows the old edge holdouts never measured, and a safety offset on the sampled windows cannot fix that. Grid verification measures every window the final check re-measures, so a pass of the plain limits (VMAF mean 88, p5 84, worst 5 s window 85, CAMBI 6/16) now goes straight to grid verification, and the grid decides. In the first production search under grid verification, a hard 1080p episode had one candidate that passed the limits at discovery (VideoToolbox q75: p5 85.9, worst window 87.3, mean 94.3) and was refused only by the margin (1.5, and 2 more for the media engine), so no verification ran. The replays in `internal/transcodeworker/benchmark_no_margin_test.go` reproduce that: with the margin it is refused with `quality_vmaf_selection_margin_not_met`; without it, it is verified on every grid window and wins only if every window passes. A thin candidate (p5 84.2) that fails a single grid window ends with no winner, and no full encode starts. The thresholds do not change.

- **Policy and compatibility.** New grid snapshots freeze `selection_margin_vmaf: 0` and no hardware extra margin, so they have new digests under the same version string: `sha256:5ca0b1d8...0976` (`allow_hardware=false`) and `sha256:1f903e42...1903` (media engine on). Snapshots frozen with verification on the grid *and* the margins keep their digests and their margins, on any worker: `sha256:6e519947...8ba8` (`allow_hardware=false`), `sha256:206ca220...03e7` (media engine on, extra margin 2), `sha256:8613e1dd...e09a` (media engine on, without the extra margin) and `sha256:ab4a33d9...6128` (media engine on, without the fallback rungs). Snapshots frozen with the grid-edge holdouts are unchanged (see above). The margin code stays in the worker for all of these; a margin of 0 means none.
- **Where the margin still applies.** The grid is what replaces the margin, so a search that cannot verify on the grid gets the margin back: the coordinator sends `selection_margin_vmaf: 1.5` (and the hardware extra margin 2, only to a worker that advertises `hardware_selection_margin`, otherwise no hardware candidates) to a worker without `automatic_grid_verification`, whose request already downgrades to the two edge holdouts, and for a source too short for the grid, which keeps the 35%/65% holdouts.
- **Rollout.** No new worker capability is needed: a margin of 0 is valid for every worker that accepts `automatic_search`, and an older worker that verifies on the grid simply applies no margin to it (the worker skips the margin when it is 0). Since the request reaching an older worker is the same shape as before, the order is not constrained, but as for every change here, **deploy the server first**: only the server decides which snapshots carry a margin. Rolling the server back is safe: both margin fields already existed in the snapshot, so new snapshots still re-hash, and they run with the margin they were frozen with (none).
- **Same-source search review.** `roundComparable` now compares the selection margin and the hardware extra margin. A round that selected with a margin (including a candidate it refused only for the margin, which passed the plain limits) is not evidence about a search without one, and the other way round, so earlier margin-only verdicts neither block a rerun nor offer only "stop".
- **Estimated size and saving for every measured setting.** `action_status.transcode.benchmark.measured[]` (and the job detail's "Measured quality and estimated size per setting") now carries `estimated_bytes`, `estimated_mb`, `savings_percent` and `larger_than_source` for each setting the search measured, passed or not, plus `source_bytes` for the original. The estimate is the one the selector already used (sampled windows, video plus audio, subtitles and container overhead); it is absent for a setting that was not measured far enough to estimate. A setting that passes the limits but would not be smaller than the original is now visible as such.

**Media-engine search (on by default for the new policy).** A synthetic-clip experiment ([2026-10-19-hardware-media-engine-experiment.md](reviews/2026-10-19-hardware-media-engine-experiment.md)) found that the VideoToolbox candidates of the size ladder produce files 2.8 to 4 times the x265 size only because they have no `gop_size`, so the encoder uses a 12-frame GOP. With `-g 240` the same quality gates give 0.86 to 1.37 times the x265 size (median about 1.0) at 4 to 7 times the encode speed and a fraction of the CPU. Size mode therefore accepts two inputs, `allow_hardware` (default **on**) and `hardware_margin_percent` (0 to 50, default 15), and freezes `size.hardware_search` in the policy while the option is on:

- The field is additive and omitted when the option is off. `allow_hardware=false` freezes a snapshot that is byte-identical to the earlier `size-v3` one, and a snapshot frozen before the option existed is reused as frozen and never re-derived, so every existing digest and every existing batch stays as it was (x265 only). A size operation that says nothing about the option, created after this change, freezes the option on, margin 15: a distinct policy with its own digest under the same version string, the same way `search_extension` and `automatic_search` were added. An operation queued but not yet frozen when this is deployed gets the default when it freezes. Quality, x265_preserve and compact modes reject `allow_hardware=true` and the margin.
- The default is visible and switchable per job or batch: the Library and batch setup show the checkbox and margin under Smaller files, *Change goal* offers the same two controls (and shows the choice that was frozen when the dialog is reopened), and the MCP inputs set it explicitly.
- A VideoToolbox winner is labeled wherever the result is shown before a replacement: the job's size result carries `encoder` and `hardware` (also in the MCP job and action detail), the web UI shows *Encoder: VideoToolbox (hardware media engine)* with a *Hardware* badge, and candidate lists name `VideoToolbox (hardware)`; the candidate-ready message says the winner is a hardware encode.
- The coordinator appends four `vt-` candidates to the size search request: `-q:v` 45, 50, 55 and 60, `gop_size` 240 and `b_frames` 1 (FFmpeg treats the encoder's B-frames as on or off, so 1 is the only enabled value), at the source's output depth. They pass the same quality gates, selection margin, quality pruning, holdout windows and savings minimum as the x265 candidates. The legacy VideoToolbox candidates of the quality and compact modes are unchanged.
- Selection: among the candidates that qualify, the smallest `vt-` result wins when it is at most `hardware_margin_percent` larger than the smallest x265 result, or when no x265 candidate qualifies. Otherwise the smallest result wins as before. The verified decision notes why a hardware candidate was chosen, and the job detail and the MCP benchmark summary show it next to the encoder. After a failed holdout, the retry to the safest `-q:v` stays inside the same margin; if no safer hardware setting is inside it, x265 is verified. When that retry verifies a safer `-q:v` than the one the selector chose, the note says so (`safer setting within the 15% margin after a failed holdout`, with the margin that was set, or `safer setting after a failed holdout; no software candidate qualified`) instead of repeating a comparison made for another setting.
- The coordinator sends `hardware_search` only to a worker that advertises `hardware_search` and the `hevc_videotoolbox` encoder, for an x265 size plan on a source long enough for the automatic search. In every other case the search stays x265-only without an error. Workers reject unknown request fields, so upgrading the server first is the compatible order, as for the earlier capability flags. The candidate cap for a search with both rules is 22 (15 rules candidates, 4 ladder rungs, 3 fallback rungs).
- **Higher-quality fallback (`hardware_search.fallback_quality_values`, default 65/70/75).** The real first trial (a hard 1080p episode) showed the ladder's best rung, q60, can fail quality (VMAF p5 78.0 against 84, worst window 81.7 against 85); pruning then skipped q45-55 and, with no rung above q60, VideoToolbox could not win and the search paid for slow x265 CRF 16 (about 0.28x). The coordinator now also appends `fallback-hardware-cand_q65/70/75` (`gop_size` 240, `b_frames` 1) when the worker advertises `hardware_fallback_search`; the worker measures them only when every initial hardware rung failed quality at discovery (measured or skipped as dominated), or the best rung failed its holdout on quality (which rules out the rungs below it). They are added to the first stage of the quality fallback (medium x265 CRF 16/18/20), not before it and not after the slow stage: the size margin needs an x265 result to compare with, and a fast hardware result then wins before the slow stage's hours of encoding. They face the same quality gates, selection margin, quality pruning (q75 first; if it fails q65 and q70 are skipped as dominated), holdout, 15% savings minimum and hardware size margin. They run only when the search reaches the quality fallback: if a ladder holdout failure leaves an eligible x265 setting, that setting is verified first and the rungs are not measured. If the medium x265 result wins on the margin and then fails its holdout on quality, every x265 setting that spends no more bits is ruled out, so the safest fallback rung is verified as "no software candidate qualified", whatever its size against the failed x265 file (the search used to end with nothing). If no medium x265 setting qualifies, a qualifying fallback rung wins as an initial rung would (the slow stage is not run to look for a smaller x265 file); if the fallback rungs fail too, the slow stage runs as before. The candidate cap of a request with `hardware_search` rises from 19 to 22 (a request still carries at most 3 fallback rungs, each one of the frozen values with the frozen GOP and B-frames, which the validator checks); what the worker measures stays bounded (3 rungs, one extra stage-sized batch of fast encodes) and the search budget is unchanged.
- The field is `omitempty`: snapshots frozen with the media engine before it (digest `sha256:bc2e370b...e03`) re-hash unchanged and run the ladder alone; new default-on snapshots have a new digest (`sha256:1b73a704...ead6`). `allow_hardware=false` is byte-identical to the earlier size policy. A worker without `hardware_fallback_search` receives neither the field nor the candidates. The capability is part of the worker's capability fingerprint, which the server recomputes from the fields it knows and fails closed on a mismatch, so **the server must be upgraded first**: a new server with an older worker matches and simply omits the rungs, while a new worker with an older server fails the fingerprint check until the server is upgraded. Rolling the server back after new default-on snapshots are frozen also fails: the older server drops `fallback_quality_values`, so those snapshots no longer re-hash to their digest. Either way the rollback has to wait until those operations finish or are re-frozen.
- **Hardware selection margin and x265 continuation.** VideoToolbox candidates now need 2 VMAF more than the 1.5 selection margin on p5 and worst window (3.5 in total: p5 at least 87.5, worst window at least 88.5 against limits of 84/85). The real trial that motivated it: `fallback-hardware-cand_q70` won the sample search on a hard 1080p episode (p5 87.1, worst window 89.2, worst frame 78.7) and then failed the whole-file check; under the extra margin it is rejected on p5. No worst-frame floor was added: the whole-file check already limits the frames below its threshold and p5, and the one frame value we have is a single episode. When a hardware winner still fails the whole-file check, the same action repeats the size search with hardware excluded (once per action, within the frozen search ceiling, reusing the worker's earlier x265 sample measurements where they match exactly) and then encodes and validates the x265 result; the failed attempt's numbers remain visible. Snapshots frozen before this change keep their digest and behavior (no extra margin); new default-on snapshots freeze `extra_selection_margin_vmaf: 2` and have a new digest (`sha256:e5ec773d...4264`); `allow_hardware=false` is unchanged. Capabilities `hardware_selection_margin` and `search_continuation` are part of the fingerprint: upgrade the server first. (Snapshots that verify on the final grid no longer freeze this extra margin; see "No selection margin when the search verifies on the grid".)
- The synthetic experiment does not establish the margin for real content; the first runs on real media should be supervised, comparing the size and quality of hardware winners with x265, and the option turned off for any batch where that comparison is unwelcome. VideoToolbox decode was not added: it is bit-identical in the experiment but gains little, and it is a follow-up behind a startup self-check.

## Calibration evidence and limits

Nine real FFmpeg encodes cover three deterministic two-second 1080p/24fps synthetic sources (motion/checker detail, SDR gradient/box, noisy native Main10), at x265 medium CRF 20/26/40. Raw measurements are in [fixtures/transcode-modes-v1.json](reviews/fixtures/transcode-modes-v1.json). The strict minimum 95 rejects the native grain q26 measurement (mean 94.3357, P5 93.0816), which static inspection showed smoothed fine noise. The size policy admits that measured compromise.
