# Transcode mode policy

`transcode_media` and `transcode_batch` accept `mode=size|quality|x265_preserve|compact` through the same action engine used by UI and MCP. Requests without `mode` run a fixed non-optimizing profile; a profile with optimization enabled, `benchmark_transcode` and the inputs `metric`, `priority`, `shared_calibration`, `calibration_items` and `batch_fixed_quality` are refused with an input error, because they only drove the removed CRF sample search. Mode requests reject profiles, quality/size controls, custom metrics and preservation overrides. Replacement authorization remains separate; filesystem operations create candidates and never replace originals.

Each operation freezes `transcode-modes-v4`, the complete policy (limits, source rules and the goal's recipe) and its SHA-256 digest. Reload verifies the persisted content against that digest. A batch child verifies its parent, manifest membership, file path and mode before inheriting the frozen policy. Plans include mode, policy digest and final size policy in their own digest. The worker still sees the plan and the final request under the version of the content v4 extends (`transcode-modes-v3` for size, `transcode-modes-v2` for the others), so the worker contract did not change with v4.

| Mode | Mean target/minimum | P5 minimum | Worst 5-second window | Final savings | Encoders (ladder rungs in [TRANSCODE_AUTOMATIC.md](TRANSCODE_AUTOMATIC.md)) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| size | 92 / 88 | 84 | 85 | at least 15%, no growth | VideoToolbox then x265 (VideoToolbox only with `allow_hardware`), or the size budget recipe |
| quality | 96 / 95 | 93 | 92 | no growth | x265 |
| x265_preserve | 96 / 95 | 93 | 92 | no growth | libx265 only |
| compact | 92 / 88 | 84 | 85 | at least 30%, no growth | x265 |

CAMBI 6/16 limits are inherited initial guardrails: the fixtures (peak at most 3.283) do not calibrate those exact boundaries.

All modes use VMAF model alias `v1_1080p_3h` (`vmaf_v1.0.16_3d0h`), enforced full-reference CAMBI mean <=6 and peak <=16, and sampled final-candidate validation. These are conservative sampled policy gates, not a guarantee of visual transparency or pixel identity. Sources already HEVC are omitted only in `x265_preserve`. Resolution, source depth/color and existing stream preservation checks remain mandatory. Supported source classes are one 8-bit or 10-bit SDR video stream below 45 fps, or progressive 45-61 fps on a worker advertising `quality.hfr_temporal_decimation` (VMAF then measures a frame-exact 2:1 decimation; see PERCEPTUAL_QUALITY_VALIDATION.md). On other workers such sources are skipped as `hfr_quality_unavailable`. Native Main10 additionally needs the worker's execution-tested native quality capability; HDR/Dolby Vision and unsupported classes preserve the original before encoding (compact converts HDR and Dolby Vision).

## Recipes: one judge, a short ladder

Earlier policies picked encode settings with a CRF search over short sample windows. It ended with nothing on most hard sources and overrated the final score of what it did pick; the whole-file final check was the only judge that held. Policy v4 therefore drops the search: every goal encodes the whole file at the first rung of a frozen ladder, most compressed first, and the final gate judges it. A quality miss moves to the next rung (one that compresses less), the first rung that passes is kept, and a rung refused for its size ends the ladder, since a better rung only makes the file larger. A rung is a function of the frozen policy, the source and the record's rung number, so validation rebuilds it and needs no other evidence. The ladders, the media engine option and the outcomes are in [TRANSCODE_AUTOMATIC.md](TRANSCODE_AUTOMATIC.md).

- **Cost.** At most one full encode and one final check per rung (three rungs per ladder; a size budget's VideoToolbox ladder has four steps). There is no search time limit and nothing is reserved against one. A batch has no shared operation cap: its cost is bounded by the files it admits times their rungs, and its parallelism by `max_parallel_jobs`.
- **Per source, no shared evidence.** Each source is encoded and judged on its own. A batch freezes one policy and its children inherit it; there is no calibration, shared quality or representative veto of an unmeasured season.
- **Encoder choice.** Numeric CRF and quality values are not compared between encoders. A size goal with the media engine allowed runs VideoToolbox first because it encodes a whole episode in minutes; a worker without VideoToolbox runs the x265 ladder, and the job says so. If every VideoToolbox rung misses quality, the record sets `retry_without_hardware` and the file may be run again with `allow_hardware=false`.
- **Audio-only fallback.** A size goal whose ladder ends with no candidate may still copy the video and compact only the lossless audio (`audio_only_fallback`), accepted when it meets the 15% minimum saving.
- **Earlier policies.** Operations frozen under `transcode-modes-v1`, `-v2` and `-v3`, and `benchmark_transcode` actions, stay readable. An old action that is not finished is ended as failed with "This operation used the removed sample search; start it again to use the current recipe. Original preserved." and nothing it planned touches the media. Their frozen digests still verify, so the history keeps showing what they were frozen with.

## Final validation windows

A size goal's final gate measures a grid of 10-second windows, one per 600 seconds of source and between 5 and 12 of them (`final_window_seconds`, `final_window_every_seconds`, `final_windows_minimum` and `_maximum` in `size`). Up to two more windows no grid window covers (`final_fresh_windows: 2`) are measured after the grid and pooled separately (`fresh_vmaf`); the verdict, the limits (VMAF mean 88, p5 84, worst window 85, CAMBI 6/16) are decided on the grid windows only. They do not gate because the limits were calibrated on grid statistics, so a gate over two extra windows would be a new, stricter limit with no calibration behind it; they exist to show whether a verified result generalizes. A fresh window that cannot be measured is dropped, not a failure. Other goals, and sources too short for the grid, measure five windows of at most 10 seconds (centered at 20%, 35%, 50%, 65% and 80%; a source under 50 seconds keeps its three planned windows). `action_status.transcode.windows` and the job detail show the score of every final window by start time.

**Media engine.** A synthetic-clip experiment ([2026-10-19-hardware-media-engine-experiment.md](reviews/2026-10-19-hardware-media-engine-experiment.md)) found that VideoToolbox output is 2.8 to 4 times the x265 size only when it has no `gop_size`, because the encoder then uses a 12-frame GOP. With `-g 240` the same quality gates give 0.86 to 1.37 times the x265 size (median about 1.0) at 4 to 7 times the encode speed and a fraction of the CPU. Size mode therefore accepts `allow_hardware` (default **on**) and freezes `size.hardware_search` (GOP 240, B-frames on) while it is on; `allow_hardware=false` freezes no hardware rules and runs x265 only. `hardware_margin_percent` is still accepted and frozen but no longer changes the result. The experiment is synthetic and does not establish quality for real content: the final gate does, per file, and the option can be turned off for any batch where a VideoToolbox result is unwelcome. A VideoToolbox result is labeled (`encoder`, `hardware`, a *Hardware* badge) wherever it is shown before a replacement.

**Size budget.** `mode=size` accepts an optional ceiling for the finished file (`size_budget_percent` 5 to 85, `size_budget_bytes` at least 50,000,000, or `size_budget_source=siblings` with `size_budget_sibling_multiplier` 1.0 to 4.0, default 1.5); the three are mutually exclusive. See "Size budget (size mode)" in [TRANSCODE_AUTOMATIC.md](TRANSCODE_AUTOMATIC.md) for the operator-facing description.

- **Effective budget.** The smaller of the budget and 85% of the source, so the 15% minimum saving the worker enforces for size mode always holds. Percent is bounded at 5 to 85 for that reason, so the refusal happens at submission rather than after an encode.
- **Siblings.** The budget is the median total bitrate of the other video files in the same folder, times the multiplier, times the source duration; the series folder above is a fallback only when the folder is named like a season, so a movie folder never widens to the library. At least 3 files of the same resolution class (SD/720p/1080p/1440p/2160p by width and 16:9-scaled height) are required; with fewer the file runs without a budget and says why. One scan probes at most 60 files within 3 minutes, and a season file is not probed again for the series. The compared files, their median, the multiplier and the source's ratio to the median are recorded (`size_budget.siblings`) and shown collapsed in the job.
- **Recipe.** With the media engine allowed and `hevc_videotoolbox` on the worker, full-file VideoToolbox encodes at `-q:v` 65, 58, 50, then the average bitrate at the budget's video bitrate (`size.size_budget.vt_quality_ladder`). Every step's plan sets `size_policy.max_candidate_bytes` to the budget; the worker refuses a larger file with `size_budget_exceeded` right after the encode and before quality scoring, and the coordinator encodes the next step. The first step that fits goes through the final validation; below the floor offers no candidate and a *Try with x265* rerun (`allow_hardware=false`, `retry_without_hardware` in the record); the last step over the budget is `size_budget_exceeded`. No capability flag is involved: a worker that predates the ceiling rejects the unknown plan field, so deploy the worker first, then the server. Otherwise (media engine off, or a worker without it) one two-pass x265 encode runs at the budget. A budget that cannot be planned (video stream size unknown, no usable bitrate left, no two-pass worker, no sibling evidence) ends the action with `size_budget_estimate_unavailable`, `size_budget_unreachable`, `size_budget_worker_unsupported` or `size_budget_unavailable` and no candidate; audio-only compaction that alone fits the budget still wins.
- **x265 encode.** Two-pass ABR, preset medium, lookahead 40, bframes 4, ref 3, aq-mode 3, aq-strength 1, psy-rd 2, psy-rdoq 1, main10 when the plan is. Target 97% of the budget; video bitrate `(target - non-video bytes) * 8 / duration`, capped at x265's 50,000 kbps, non-video bytes being the source minus its video stream minus the lossless-audio compaction saving. Validation rebuilds the settings, preset, target and bitrate from the frozen policy and the source report and requires an exact match.
- **Validation floor.** The same final windows, with a frozen floor of VMAF mean 70, p5 60 and worst 5 s window 65. About 20 VMAF spans one ACR category and about 6 VMAF is one JND, so this is roughly four JNDs below the normal 88/84/85 gates. CAMBI is not lowered: banding is the characteristic low-bitrate artefact on dark content, and a floor on VMAF alone would not catch it.
- **Outcomes.** `meets_quality_target_within_budget` and `below_quality_target_within_budget` (labelled *Below quality target — within size budget* in the job, queue, batch candidate list and replacement review, and counted in the batch approval's prompt); a quality rejection when the floor is missed; `size_budget_exceeded` when the file is still over budget, including an overshoot past the 15% minimum saving on either side. The summary reports `plan_source=size_budget`. A budget candidate is never auto-promoted.
- **Policy and compatibility.** `size.size_budget` is `omitempty`: with no budget the frozen policy carries none. The UI and the `transcode_goal_change` tool carry the budget (`size_budget_*` inputs) through *Change goal* and *Run again*, and the preview shows the budget each selected attempt was frozen with.
- **Not done.** The floor is frozen and not user-tunable.

## Calibration evidence and limits

Nine real FFmpeg encodes cover three deterministic two-second 1080p/24fps synthetic sources (motion/checker detail, SDR gradient/box, noisy native Main10), at x265 medium CRF 20/26/40. Raw measurements are in [fixtures/transcode-modes-v1.json](reviews/fixtures/transcode-modes-v1.json). The strict minimum 95 rejects the native grain q26 measurement (mean 94.3357, P5 93.0816), which static inspection showed smoothed fine noise. The size policy admits that measured compromise. Motion q26 measured mean 96.4376/P5 95.1632 and retained the checker detail in the inspected crop. Motion q40 measured mean 77.9165 and lost detail; gradient q40 measured mean 86.3582 and showed ringing, both rejected by the size minimum. Gradient q20/q26 remain below the strict minimum, so the strict policy conservatively has no winner among those tested settings.

The reviewer inspected selected native-resolution crops at t=1s: motion source/q26/q40, gradient source/q26/q40 and grain source/q20/q26. This was AI static-image inspection, not human full-motion review or validation against a real library. Small synthetic samples cannot establish universal perceptual thresholds. FFV1 reference-byte savings are not representative of H264/HEVC library savings. No production source was encoded or replaced. These measurements substantiate the bounded starting policy and its conservative grain rejection; they do not demonstrate an operational speed improvement.

Local system FFmpeg 9.0.1/libvmaf could not initialize the requested model (`Speed_chroma_feature_speed_chroma_uv_score`). Measurement used a temporary libvmaf build pinned to `f85a853692a8c730d0270cd733c8bb30b5b93b7c`; no system library was replaced. Workers independently execution-probe model availability and fail closed when unavailable. To reproduce with a compatible installed libvmaf or a separately built pinned library:

```sh
python3 scripts/calibrate_transcode_modes.py --output-dir /tmp/navigatorr-mode-calibration --ffmpeg /path/to/ffmpeg --libvmaf-dir /path/to/vmaf-build/src
```

The optional library directory only affects subprocesses launched by the reproducer. Omitting it uses the normal FFmpeg installation. The script emits the references, candidates, per-frame metric logs, screenshots and JSON measurements in the requested output directory; timings are host-specific.
